Wells Fargo · GenAI complaint classification

Senior Assistant Vice President · 2025–2026

Product challenge

Manual complaint categorization took 3–5 minutes per case and operated inside a highly regulated consumer-operations environment where model errors could not be treated equally across categories.

My role

Owned the initiative from problem discovery and requirements through POC, evaluation, validation and production-readiness review with operations stakeholders.

Key product decisions

  • Evaluate quality by category, not just headline accuracy, so low-volume high-risk fraud failures were visible.
  • Route lower-confidence outputs to humans instead of forcing full automation.
  • Log model outputs, confidence and decision paths to support explainability and auditability.
Outcome: 95% test accuracy, under 2 seconds per complaint, and a USD $3M projected annual-savings business case for a 160-person operations team.

Diageo · Pricing platform and automation

Assistant Manager, Pricing & Analytics · 2022–2025

Product challenge

Pricing workflows across SAP and Anaplan depended on manual reconciliation and fragmented controls across APAC and Australia, creating operational effort and financial risk.

My role

Product owner for the internal pricing platforms: roadmap, backlog, release planning, prioritization and stakeholder alignment across finance, commercial and IT.

Key product decisions

  • Prioritize the backlog using impact, effort and regulatory/operational risk rather than stakeholder volume.
  • Automate SAP-to-Anaplan reconciliation while keeping finance validation and governed controls visible.
  • Instrument adoption and variance-control KPIs so release value could be measured after launch.
Outcome: AUD $12M in annual value validated by finance, 2–3 FTE released from manual work, 35% improvement in pricing accuracy/control effectiveness and 60% reduction in pricing-operations cycle time.

Agentic AI Compliance Assistant · Public prototype

Independent build · 2026

Product question

How should an AI system decide when it is safe to act autonomously, when a human should review the output, and when the task should be escalated?

Current state

Working prototype with mock-mode support. It is a portfolio and architecture demonstration, not a production-hardened compliance product.

What the prototype demonstrates

  • A 6-factor risk engine gating autonomous, review-required and escalate outcomes.
  • Plan–Execute–Reflect orchestration with quality-threshold replanning and a full audit trail.
  • Counterfactual what-if analysis, human oversight and 8 curated risk scenarios.
Product takeaway: autonomy is a product policy, not just a model capability. The risk threshold, human fallback and audit trail belong in the product design.

How I work

Four product principles I keep returning to.

They come from working in regulated, operationally complex environments where an AI demo is easy and a trustworthy product is not.

Start with the workflow.

Understand the user decision, handoffs, failure cost and business metric before choosing the model or architecture.

Evaluate by failure mode.

A single accuracy number hides the cases that matter most. Segment quality and define release bars by risk.

Design human oversight.

Uncertainty should change the workflow. Confidence thresholds, review routes and escalation are product behaviors.

Measure business value.

Ship with success metrics: time saved, control improvement, adoption, error reduction, cost and operational outcomes.
