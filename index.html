GenAI complaint classification in a regulated bank: what the evaluation changed

Wells Fargo · GenAI POC · May 2025 to Feb 2026

Problem. A 160-person U.S. consumer-operations team categorized complaints by hand, 3 to 5 minutes each, with inconsistent results that mattered for CFPB reporting.

Decision 1: evaluate per category, not on headline accuracy. Overall test accuracy reached 95%. Broken out by category, precision and recall on low-volume fraud categories were well below the rest, and those are the highest-stakes complaints. The release criterion became a per-category quality bar.

Decision 2: route by confidence, per category. Above the threshold the system classifies; below it, an analyst does. High-stakes categories got tighter thresholds and more human review.

Decision 3: log everything. Every classification records the prompt version, the model output and the confidence, so any decision can be explained to a regulator after the fact.

Outcome. 95% test accuracy, under 2 seconds per complaint, USD $3M projected annual savings in analyst time. Validated for production readiness.

What I would do differently. Design the per-category evaluation on day one, and bring model-risk stakeholders into discovery instead of validation.
